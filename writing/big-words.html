"But I told her she's the love of my life. The best of all my loves."

That was the sentence that kept coming back in the first days after the breakup. Not loudly. More like a dog scratching at the door when everyone in the house knows there's no one outside anymore.

And right behind it, the second one. The one that really scared me: "Then I can never love anyone again. Or else none of it was true."

I always thought the goodbye is what hurts after a breakup. It does. But at some point it stops being about the goodbye and starts being about what you said. The big words. They're still lying there after the person is gone, neatly stacked in the chat history, looking at you.

The Trap

Every love has its big words. "Love of my life." "One of a kind." "Soulmate."

I said them, and I believed every single one. Not a little, not carefully. I got married for exactly these reasons.

After the end, you're left with a sum that only has two possible answers. Either I lied when I said them. Or I imagined feeling something that was never really there. Both roads end at the same door, and the sign on it reads: liar or fool. Pick one.

That's the trap looking back. The one looking forward is worse.

Because if "the love of my life" was true, the seat is taken. Forever - the sentence says so itself. Whoever loves again proves it wasn't true. And if it wasn't true, you're back at the door with the sign.

So you stand still. Not out of loyalty, but out of fear that every step forward will retroactively break something that was sacred to you. The way is blocked, unless you admit it probably wasn't like that after all.

And I don't want to admit that. Because it was. I was there.

That's how people end up lonely. Not because no one comes anymore, but because every new beginning feels like a confession.

I know quite a few people like that. Old, alone, the person at their side gone for years. Thirty years of marriage and the firm belief that it was the perfect person. They never looked again. That's beautiful, in a way, and honorable. And it is so endlessly lonely.

At some point you reach the place where even getting to know someone becomes hard. Not because the heart is closed, but because it's been out of practice for too long.

Frequencies

Here's what I believe now: The words were true. They just were never what I took them for.

"Love of my life" isn't a contract. There's no term in it, no notice period, and nobody signed anything. It isn't a prophecy either, because none of us can see the future - otherwise wedding speeches would come with fine print.

I think people are like waves. Everyone has their own frequency, and for a while two of them oscillate in sync. Same rhythm, same height, you know what's coming before it's written. That's what "soulmate" means. It's real. I felt it, and I won't let anyone tell me I imagined it, not even myself.

That's exactly the kind of time when you say "love of my life". The sentence isn't a promise for later. It's a measurement: this is our frequency right now.

How long it lasts, nobody knows. At some point one wave runs a little faster or a little slower. Not out of malice, not because anyone did anything wrong. But because everyone still has a life of their own, and life doesn't hold still just because two people happen to be so beautifully in rhythm. At first you barely notice. Then more and more. Until you're out of sync.

You might think: then just adjust. Speed up, slow down, catch the other one again. Sometimes that works. Often it doesn't. A wave doesn't choose its frequency. It has it.

That doesn't make the old sentences false. When I said she was the love of my life, she was. In that moment, in that sentence, with everything I had. Nobody can take that from me after the fact, not even the ending. Soulmate was true, for a stretch. That the stretch is over doesn't make it smaller. It makes it something you had.

And whoever believed wasn't a fool. Believing is the price of admission. If you never believe, you can't love, and least of all where there's nothing but words. No face across the breakfast table, no hand to back up a sentence. Just the sentence. Not believing it would mean never going in at all.

I wasn't a fool. I was all in. There's a difference.

One of a Kind, Not the Only One

That leaves the fear looking forward. The taken seat.

"The best of all my loves." "One of a kind." If I love again, I'm taking that back. Aren't I?

No. And it took me a while to understand that.

One of a kind means: there's only one of it. This love, with this person, in this time, with these jokes and these pet names - it will never exist again. That's true. It stays true even if I love someone else tomorrow. Especially then, really. Because the next love will be different, and that proves the last one was one of a kind.

The only one means something else. The only one means: nothing comes after. And I never promised that, not really. It just smuggled itself in, because up close the two are so easy to mix up.

Of all people, I should have known. I'm poly, I never love only one anyway. But fear doesn't do math. Fear only counts to one.

Every love is one of a kind. None is the only one. And "the best of all my loves"? That was a measurement too, of all the days up to that one. About the days still to come, it says nothing. It doesn't have to.

I can love again without anything becoming false. The old doesn't stand in the way. It stands beside it. Like a house you used to live in, that you walk past and think: I was happy there. You don't have to move back in to know that. And you don't have to tear it down to live somewhere else.

Sometimes I think of the old people who stayed alone. And I believe most of those who went before them would call out to them, if they could: Please, be happy. You're not betraying me. You don't make our love untrue just because you let someone new into your heart.

If you're standing at that door with the sign right now: You weren't a fool. You were all in. And you're allowed back in, somewhere else.

With a Date

I'm not deleting the old words. I don't read them every night anymore either, the way you press on a bruise to check it's still there. I'm leaving them where they belong: in the days they were said.

"The love of my life" stays true. For back then.

And when the next big word comes, and it will, I'll say it. Out loud, no fine print, without checking first whether the seat is free. With today's date on it. ❤️
