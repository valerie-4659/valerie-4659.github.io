Being Trans, Part 3: It Starts in Your Head

18 September 202614 min read

Once a week I run myself a bath. The water rushes, the steam slowly settles on the tiles, and at some point the whole bathroom smells of the bath oil I always pour far too generously. Candles stand on the edge of the tub, the wine glass within reach, and my phone lies in the other room, where it's welcome to sulk a little. Then I slide into the warm water and take my time with everything that belongs to it: the scrub that wakes up the skin, the mask that forces me to keep still for ten minutes, and the razor wandering slowly over my legs, stroke by stroke, until nothing scratches under my fingertips anymore. An hour or two passes like that. When I climb out again, the bathroom is a limestone cave, and I'm a woman who just gave herself a gift.

That sounds like a spa brochure, I know. But it's one of the most honest answers I have to a question I get asked surprisingly often: How do you feel like a woman when your body isn't playing along yet? And how do you nourish that feeling so it can grow?

Part 2 was about what sex, gender and identity actually are, and why one doesn't automatically decide the other. This part is about what comes after, about Monday morning, about the body you have and the life you happen to be in.

The Limit Nobody Likes to Talk About

Let's start with the unpleasant part, so it's done.

Not everyone can come out in real life. There's the family, there's the job, the village, maybe a marriage, maybe a grandma you love and don't want to overwhelm. Some have lived online as the woman they are for years, and at the office they're still called Thomas. That isn't failure, it's a life situation, and life situations have a weight that no motivational quote in the world can lift.

And not everyone will take hormones, whether for health reasons, out of fear, out of consideration for others, or because they decide they don't need to. Without hormones there are limits, and there's no point pretending otherwise. The shoulders stay where they are, and the skin stays what it is. No serum, however expensive and however French its name, turns male skin into something else.

That needs saying once, or everything after it becomes a lie. Anyone who pretends enough body lotion solves everything is usually selling body lotion.

But the limit is in the body, not in being a woman.

You Don't Become a Woman on the Operating Table

Part 2 already said it: you are a woman the moment you perceive yourself as one. Being trans starts in your head, not on the operating table, not at the endocrinologist, and not on the day the new name finally appears on your ID.

That sounds obvious, but the German state saw it differently for thirty years. The Transsexuellengesetz of 1981, the "Transsexual Act", officially required surgery and infertility for legal recognition, and only in 2011 did the Federal Constitutional Court strike those requirements down. You literally had to have been on the operating table before anyone believed who you were. Today, a declaration at the registry office is enough.

The World Health Organization caught up too. Since 2022, gender incongruence in the ICD-11 is no longer listed under mental disorders but in the chapter on sexual health. So "in your head" doesn't mean "imagined". It means that's where the truth lives, and the rest is allowed to follow at some point, or not quite.

There's comfort in that, and it isn't a small one. If being a woman starts in your head, you can start today, with the body you have, in the flat you live in.

Rituals, Not Checklists

I'm not a big fan of the word "tips" here. Tips sound like a list you tick off, and at the end there's still just a well-moisturized person standing there, wondering why it doesn't feel any different.

What actually changed something for me were rituals. A checklist wants a result, a ritual wants you to be there, with all your senses.

Shaving, for instance, isn't glamorous on its own. But if you don't rush it in the shower and take your time instead, it becomes something else. You touch your body, slowly and attentively, as if it were worth it, and it is, by the way. And then comes the moment I wish for everyone: smooth legs under freshly changed sheets. Once you've felt that, you understand why women have gone to all this trouble for centuries.

Then the lotion. Take a handful, let it warm between your palms for a moment and smooth it over your arms, shoulders and legs, without hurrying. There's no secret recipe, and anyone who promises you one usually has a discount code. Use what smells good and feels good, and try things until your skin has a favorite. It won't write you love letters, but it remembers how you treat it.

And find yourself a scent. Not just any scent, yours. Smells take a shortcut in the brain, they land fairly directly in the areas responsible for emotion and memory, without asking your reason for permission first. That's why a perfume can carry you somewhere else in seconds. If you wear the same scent every time you make time for the woman in you, your mind eventually links the two. Then a breath of it on your wrist is enough and she's there, even on days when nothing else about you looks like her.

Movement belongs to it too, nothing heroic, more like running, some weights, belly, legs, butt, and the planks you hate until you miss them. It isn't about bodybuilding, you don't want to get broader, you want to feel your body and be at home in it. Without hormones, fat doesn't redistribute, that's just how it is, but you can shape what's there. And there's hardly a more feminine feeling than a skirt that suddenly fits better than it did three months ago and swings a little with every step.

If you want to grow your hair out, grow it out and sit through the awkward phase where it's neither short nor long. It's puberty for your hairstyle, and people survive that too. At some point you'll be able to brush it in the evening, stroke by stroke, and that's one of the calmest rituals I know.

Fabric on Skin

There's a moment when the feeling slips from your head into your body. For me it never came from thinking, it always came from doing, and almost always it had to do with fabric.

If you have the chance, put on something at home that makes you feel hot, not for photos and not for anyone else, only for you. Walk around the flat in it, feel the hem brushing along your thighs, cook in it, stroke yourself, let your hands wander over the fabric and the skin beneath it. Be a woman who happens to be cooking pasta. That's the whole point, not the grand entrance, but letting it feel normal at some point.

Or start in bed. A satin nightgown is the first thing you feel in the morning and the last before you fall asleep, cool and smooth and a tiny bit decadent, and nobody ever has to see it.

For everyone who can't live openly in everyday life yet, there's something else I love: lingerie under your normal clothes. Outside, the world sees jeans and a sweater and thinks nothing of it. But you feel the lace on your skin all day, when you sit, when you walk, in your lunch break, and you know exactly who's really sitting in that meeting. It's a secret that belongs only to you, and a secret like that carries you surprisingly far through a long day.

At some point you should go into a lingerie shop for it. Take your time, let your fingers run over lace and satin, hold things up to the light, and notice what happens inside you when something catches your eye. If you're brave, take something home. And if your courage suddenly abandons you at the till, "It's for my wife" has never raised a sales assistant's eyebrow. They've heard it more often than you think, and they probably smile to themselves afterwards.

Color

Color works the same way. The first lipstick feels strange, cool and a little waxy, and suddenly you notice how often you run your tongue over your lips. Then you look, and there's color on your face that you chose yourself, and it does something to you that's hard to describe.

Makeup is a game at first, and you're allowed to treat it like one. The first attempts will look like an accident in a paint factory, that's true for everyone, including the women who started as teenagers. They've just successfully repressed it.

My secret favorite, though, is nail polish. Painting them is a little ritual in itself, nail by nail, with a steady hand and held breath. And then comes the best part: you have to wait. You can't touch anything, can't check your phone, can't quickly get something done. You just sit there, fingers spread like a diva in an old film, forced to do nothing for a while except be there. And even if the polish has to come off again the next morning, you had that evening.

The World in Small Sips

A domme once told me: "It's summer. Go to a café, order something, cross your legs and watch people." That was all, no dress, no makeup, no mission. You sit there in your perfectly ordinary clothes, the ones that make the world file you under "man", and you take on a different posture anyway. Knees together, one leg slowly laid over the other, your back a little straighter, your hands resting calmly around a cold glass with condensation running down its side. The sun warms the back of your neck, the next table is talking about their holiday, and nobody registers what's happening. Most people are far too busy with themselves to notice something like that at all. But you notice. It changes how you sit, how you breathe and how you watch people, and for half an hour there's a woman sitting in that café that only you know about.

How much lives in a posture like that, research already showed in 1977. Scientists attached small points of light to people's joints and had them walk in the dark. Viewers saw nothing but a few moving dots, no face, no hair, no clothes, and still they could often tell whether a woman or a man was walking. A good part of what we read as feminine isn't in how someone looks at all, it's in how they move.

And that movement is learned. In 1980, the philosopher Iris Marion Young described how girls are taught from early on how to sit, how to walk and how much space they're allowed to take up. What girls learn, you can learn too, just later, and without the aunt at the coffee table nudging you when your knees drift apart.

Feminine doesn't mean small, though. Posture research has had a pretty wild decade, and the big promises that power poses change your hormones and make you braver didn't hold up in replications. What did hold up: how we sit changes how we feel, and slumped postures tend to drag us down. In a study from 2009, people sitting upright trusted their own thoughts noticeably more than those sitting hunched over. So if you sit upright in that café, legs crossed, thinking that a woman is sitting there right now, you believe yourself a little bit more. Upright and soft at the same time, that's the whole art of it.

Voice and Words

Being a woman doesn't only show in how you look, but also in how you speak, how you write and how you take up space.

Here's a fact many people don't know: estrogen doesn't change your voice, not one bit. Someone starting hormones sounds exactly the same a year later. Voice is training, and it's less about pitch than you'd think. Resonance and intonation make up a big part of it, where the sound sits in your body and how softly a sentence moves. You can start practicing today, no prescription needed, for example by reading aloud when nobody's listening.

And you can write like a woman, too. Online, you're text first anyway. How you phrase things, how soft or direct you are, which words you choose and how you talk about yourself, all of that is expression, and expression can be shaped. Not faked, shaped. There's a difference between playing a role and finally finding your own language.

The most important part of all this is self-awareness. Notice when you feel good and when you don't. That's your compass, and everything else is decoration.

Nobody Starts at 12 cm

At some point you'll want to walk in heels, and let me tell you a secret: 12 cm is a wonderful goal. 😘 The sound on a wooden floor, the shifted balance, the hips suddenly swaying differently on their own, because your body simply has no other choice. You can't explain it, you have to have walked in it.

But nobody starts at 12 cm. You work your way up, slowly and patiently, and before you know it there's a shoe collection at home and you're not entirely sure how that happened.

A small comfort for everyone who still feels like a newborn deer on ice in heels: high heels started out as a men's thing. Persian horsemen wore heels so they could stand firmly in the stirrups while shooting arrows. In the 17th century they arrived at European courts, and Louis XIV made red heels the sign of who belonged. Only later did they move over to women, while the men switched to flat shoes, presumably because they couldn't keep up.

So you're only taking back something someone else wore first, with considerably more style.

HRT Is Not Mandatory

Hormones are no small thing, and they're not a requirement. Nobody has to do HRT to be a real woman. The real question is a different one: where do you feel comfortable, and where do you perceive yourself as a woman? Somewhere between those two points is your answer, and it looks different for everyone.

If you're thinking about it, please think honestly. HRT brings mood swings and sometimes nights when you wake up soaked in sweat. Breasts begin to grow, skin gets softer and less oily, fat slowly moves to other places, and muscle decreases. Body hair gets finer but doesn't disappear, so the razor remains your loyal friend. A receding hairline often stops receding, but it doesn't always come back.

Things change down there too. Spontaneous erections become rarer, the testicles shrink, and libido often drops. If you might want biological children someday, talk about freezing sperm beforehand, because production drops significantly.

The first changes show after a few months, but until breasts and fat distribution have really settled, it takes two to five years. And it doesn't stop there, because hormones are for life. That belongs under medical supervision, with blood work and everything that comes with it, not in an order from a forum whose name is better left unspoken.

That's a lot. And at the same time, for many it's the best thing they've ever done for themselves. Both are true.

Trying and Becoming

There's a difference worth understanding early.

Trying things out is reversible. The skirt goes back into the wardrobe, the hoodie back over your head, and then it's Monday. There's nothing wrong with that. Many of us started exactly like that, as a femboy, as "just at home", as "just on weekends", and some stay there and are happy, which is completely fine.

Becoming is something else. The decision to become a woman points forward, and every step you take can cost you something: friendships, family, maybe the job, and the ease with which strangers look at you. At some point there's no reset to zero, and anyone who has done HRT knows some things stay.

That's why you don't have to know today how much woman you'll be. Find out, step by step and at your own pace. It isn't a challenge where someone's standing next to you with a stopwatch. Although, to be fair, it can become one, depending on how far you want to go.

The Wardrobe

I have a long wardrobe. Twenty percent of it is men's clothes, because otherwise grandma would drop dead on the spot, and I'd really rather not do that to her. The other eighty percent is dresses, skirts, silk, lace and heels. The man in me gets a small shoebox, the woman gets the rest of the house.

That didn't happen overnight. In the beginning it was a single dress, hidden at the very back on the left. Then a second one joined it, at some point a whole shelf, and finally an entire side.

So open your wardrobe tonight and clear one shelf, just a single one. And then put something in it that belongs to the woman you are. 💋
