When Someone Feels More Than You Do

You're already in bed, the light is off, and your phone lights up one more time. A message, and even in the preview you can see it's a long one. Something in you knows before you open it.

You read it once. Then again, slower. The third time you're not really reading anymore, just staring at the one sentence in the middle. It stands there like a guest who arrived at the party too early and doesn't know what to do with their hands: "I think I've fallen in love with you."

And you go warm and cold at the same time. Warm, because it's lovely to be seen like that. Cold, because you already know you don't feel the same. And because in a moment you'll have to write something that will hurt a person you're actually very fond of.

There are whole libraries about unrequited love. Almost all of it is written from the side of the ones who love. This text is for the other side. For you, when you're loved and can't love back - and right now you don't know whether that makes you the villain.

There's no answer that doesn't hurt

Let's start with the uncomfortable part: this can't be done painlessly. There's no wording, no perfect moment and no emoji that turns a no into something soft. Believe me, I looked. 🙈

That's not about your choice of words. It's because someone wished for something you don't have. A wish that doesn't come true hurts, no matter how prettily you wrap it. A rejection with a bow on it is still a rejection. It just has a bow.

So the goal isn't a painless no. The goal is an honest one. One after which the other person knows where they stand, instead of spending months reading signs that were never signs.

A guilty conscience isn't guilt

And then it arrives. The guilty conscience. It sits down on your bed, pulls the blanket off you and asks whether you didn't do something wrong after all. Were you too warm? Too playful? Should you have noticed sooner? Are you now the reason someone isn't sleeping tonight?

It's worth keeping apart two things that like to walk around in the same coat. Guilt requires that you did something wrong. A guilty conscience only requires that someone matters to you.

You didn't make anyone fall in love. Feelings happen, they aren't ordered, and certainly not by you. Someone liked you, came closer, and at some point tripped over a line you never drew. That's not an offense. That's just life happening in a place you can't follow.

Guilt requires that you did something wrong. A guilty conscience only requires that someone matters to you.

Feelings aren't a contract. Nobody has to sign for a delivery they never ordered. You're allowed to hand the parcel back kindly without apologizing to the mail carrier.

You can keep the guilty conscience anyway. It shows you're not cold. But don't let it decide. It's a good guest and a terrible advisor.

The three shortcuts

When the no sits heavy in your stomach, shortcuts offer themselves. They all look kind. None of them are.

The soft no. "I don't know what I'm feeling right now." "Maybe someday." "Let's just see where it goes." It sounds gentle, but it's the cruelest version if you actually already know. For someone in love, a maybe isn't a no with padding. It's a yes in installments. People live on that for months, and in the end the bill is higher than any clear rejection.

Sometimes, though, it really is just too early. Or it doesn't fit into your life right now, because there's already too much going on, the strength isn't there, or the moment is simply wrong. Nobody gets to choose that. Then you're allowed to say exactly that. The difference from the soft no is honesty: "Not right now, and I can't promise you that will change" is an answer. "We'll see" is a waiting room without a clock.

The pity yes. You don't want to make anyone sad, so you slide into something you don't feel. That's not a gift, that's a costume. And costumes get noticed. Not right away, but at some point the replies get shorter and the hearts fewer. Then it hurts twice as much, because on top of the no comes the feeling of having been politely lied to for weeks.

Making yourself small. "Oh, you deserve someone better." "I'm way too complicated anyway." It feels modest, but it's an escape. You turn your no into a verdict on yourself so you don't have to stand by it. And then the other person gets to comfort you on top of everything. It's roughly like stepping on someone's foot and then wailing so loudly about your own shoe that they go and fetch you a band-aid.

And then there's the shortcut of going very quiet and hoping the feeling wanders off on its own. That one deserves its own text. Just this much: it doesn't wander off. It waits.

"But you have room"

If you live poly, you know a special edition of this situation. The other person knows you love more than one person. And somewhere in the message, spoken or not, sits the question: Then why not me too?

Because poly means you can love more than one person. Not that you have to love everyone who knocks. A heart with several rooms is still not a hotel where anyone can just check in at reception. 🏨

Love happens or it doesn't, monogamous or poly. The number of rooms doesn't change who gets a key. And honestly, the space isn't endless either. Time, attention and energy are the real rooms, and they fill up faster than you'd think.

"I have room in my life for you, but not in this form" is a complete sentence. It doesn't need an appendix explaining why the others got a yes and this person got a no.

The no itself

So how do you say it? There's no script, but a few things help.

Early. As soon as you know. Not after three days of brooding over whether it might come after all. Every day of waiting is a day someone spends hoping. If you need time to think, say exactly that: "I've read your message, it means a lot to me, and I'll answer you properly tomorrow." That's not a maybe. That's an appointment.

Clear. The one sentence that matters has to be in there, and it can't hide between three paragraphs of affection: "I don't feel the same way about you." No "kind of", no "right now", no "I think" if you know.

Warm. Clear doesn't mean cold. You're allowed to say what this person means to you. You're allowed to say it moves you to be seen like that. And you're allowed to say thank you. Someone just showed you something very vulnerable. That deserves respect, even if you can't accept it.

No novel of reasons. You don't have to explain why you're not in love. Usually there isn't even a reason you could write down. Long explanations also read like a list of things that just need fixing. And then someone starts working on the list.

No belittling the feeling. "It's probably just a phase" or "You don't even really know me" takes away someone's right to their own heart. You don't have to return the love. But you don't have to declare it invalid either.

In text you have an advantage you'd never have face to face: you can write, delete and write again. Use it. Just not until dawn.

At some point your thumb hovers over the screen like a skydiver counting one more time. The jump doesn't get easier the longer you stand at the door. ❤️

Keeping the person

Now the part you probably want most: that everything stays the way it was. Just without the feeling. Friendship, totally normal, memes again tomorrow.

The uncomfortable truth is that you don't decide that alone. You can leave the door open. Whether someone walks through it, when and how, gets decided on the other side.

Some people need distance. A few days, a few weeks, sometimes longer. That's not withdrawing love and it's not punishment, it's wound care. Someone who just fell in love with you can't giggle with you the next day about your latest crush. If it goes quiet, let it be quiet. Don't keep knocking to check whether it's okay yet. Every "Are we okay?" presses on exactly the spot that's trying to heal.

And then there's the matter of comforting. You notice someone is hurting, and everything in you wants to rush over and help. But you can't be the band-aid for the wound your no opened. That's like the rain offering to dry the laundry. Well meant, physically tricky. ☔ For this kind of comfort, other people are better suited than you right now.

What you can do: stay reliable. Don't disappear, don't suddenly act like a stranger. And if the message comes up at some point, don't pretend it was never written.

Also take an honest look at which habits between you are now feeding hope you can't fulfill. What used to be a game might read like a promise on the other side right now.

Nobody knows yet what will come of it. Sometimes a no turns into a friendship for life. Sometimes, years later, the friendship becomes more after all, because life rearranged itself or two people grew towards each other. And sometimes the friendship quietly dissolves at some point, without anyone having done anything wrong. Feelings don't keep to timetables. 🚂

Just don't build on it. Not you, by sending a no with a back door, and not the other person, by staying on the platform waiting for a train that may never run. What comes later comes later. And however it turns out: it's not your fault.

Send

The tea on the nightstand went cold long ago. The message is still there, and the little "read" underneath feels louder than usual.

You sit up and switch on the little lamp. You write the one sentence that has to be in there. Then the one that says what this person means to you. Then thank you. You delete a "kind of". You delete a "maybe".

Then you hit send. What comes next is no longer up to you. You put the phone face down on the nightstand and turn off the light.
